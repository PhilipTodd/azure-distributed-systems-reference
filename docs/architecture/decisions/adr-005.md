# ADR-005: Tracking Cosmos partition key

Version: 0.2 | Date: 20 October 2026 | Status: Accepted

Owner/approver: human owner/architect. Evidence: “I agree with /tankId as partition key” and the instruction to update documentation, recorded in [20 October decisions](../../engineering/authorised-decisions-2026-10-20.md).

## Context and options

Tank-centred queries and local panel/state/outbox atomicity favour a tank partition. Alternatives such as tank plus time bucket or hierarchical partitioning can distribute growth but complicate historical queries, maintenance records and transaction placement.

## Decision

Use /tankId for the proposed Tracking history container and other tank-scoped Tracking containers introduced under an approved design. Aquarium owners retain globally unique tank IDs. Tracking containers live within the platform-owned ReferenceProjects Cosmos database (ADR-024).

Measurement panel, idempotency and related outbox documents must be in the same container with the same tankId value for one Cosmos transactional batch. Identical partition-key values across different containers do not enable a cross-container transaction. Do not separate a panel's outbox into a second container.

This is physical data routing, not authorisation. API/worker identities have scoped data access and resource-level owner checks.

## Consequences and verification

Target tank queries with the partition key. Test representative long-lived tank growth, hotspots, indexing/query cost and transaction limits before production. One large/hot tank concentrates traffic/storage; record capacity headroom and migration options. Acceptance is not a claim that workload tests passed.

Lease/checkpoint or future cross-tank operational stores need their own explicit design; do not invent tank IDs to fit unrelated data. Exact container names/count, indexes, TTL, retention and capacity settings remain TBD. Throughput belongs to the shared-platform owner.
